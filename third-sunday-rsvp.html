Why we're doing this

It's easy to let months slide by without really seeing each other. So the plan is simple: one morning a month, we meet up, eat, and take a walk. It's just family for now, and later on I'd like to open it up to friends too. The kids get to grow up knowing their cousins, and the rest of us actually get to catch up.

Come when you can. If you miss a month, no worries, we'll be back the next one.

Health & keeping each other safe

We'll have babies, toddlers, and grandparents at the table, and some of them can't fight much off. Please keep that in mind so this stays safe for everyone.

Feeling sick?
If you're sick or feel something coming on, stay home and catch us next month. Better to miss one breakfast than pass something around a table full of little kids.
Vaccines
Think about your vaccination status before you come. It's not only about you. The infants and older folks sitting next to you are the ones most at risk.
The ask
Everyone who comes needs to be current on the CDC's recommended vaccine schedule for their age. If you're not, please sit out until you are. This part isn't flexible. We've got kids here too young to be fully protected yet, and I won't put them at risk.
I'd rather say this plainly here than have it come up at the park. It comes down to keeping the little ones safe.

How the morning goes

  • 🍳
    8:30 – 10:00 · Breakfast
    Scrambled eggs, chorizo, fruit, and coffee at our tables. We've got the food covered, so just show up hungry anytime after 8:30. No need to be on time.
  • 🌳
    10:00 – 11:00 · Lake walk
    An easy, flat loop around the lake at a slow pace, fine for strollers. Do all of it or part of it. We start and end at the tables.
  • 🛝
    11:00 · Playground & goodbyes
    Back at the tables. Kids play, the adults finish their coffee, and people head out whenever they're ready.
The whole thing runs about two and a half hours, but you don't have to stay for all of it. Come and go as your day allows.

Park hours
Opens 7 AM daily. Closing time changes by season:March through October: 7 AM to 8 PM. November through February: 7 AM to 5 PM. The last car is let in 30 minutes before closing. Our morning falls well inside those hours either way.

The booth only takes cards: debit, credit, prepaid, or phone pay (Apple/Google Pay). No cash. If cash is all you've got, buy a day ticket at the Park Office just past the booth, or at the Nature Center. Walk or bike in and you don't pay at all.

The annual pass

If you're planning to come most months, a yearly pass is worth it. At $8 a visit, it pays for itself in about nine Sundays.

Buy one at the El Dorado Nature Center Visitors Center (7550 E Spring St), open Tuesday through Sunday, 8:30 AM to 4 PM. They take cash, check, or card. It's one pass per car, kept on the dashboard, so if two families drive in separately, each car needs its own. When you buy it, they knock that day's entrance fee off the price.

Good to know

Easy for all
The paths are flat and paved, so strollers and wheelchairs are no problem. Works for grandparents and little kids alike.
Restrooms
There are public restrooms near the picnic areas, so no long walk when someone needs to go.
For the kids
There's a playground right by the tables, plus ducks and the lake to watch. The park also has paddleboats and bike rentals if anyone wants to stick around.
Weather
Mornings near the coast can start cool and gray before it warms up, so bring a layer.
Rain
If it's raining, we won't meet that day. Keep an eye on the forecast, and I'll send word if we need to call one off.
Dogs
Leashed dogs are fine on the paths, and there's a dog park nearby if yours needs to run around.
